# Monster Battle 9001: Voice Script

Every spoken line in the game, grouped by the moment that triggers it. There are 181 lines for 34 moments ("cues").

This page is the readable copy for review. The copy the game and the voice generator actually use is the script data file `shared/src/defs/voiceLines.json`. If the two ever disagree, the data file is right and this page needs regenerating.

## How it works

- The game announces a **cue** when something happens (a volley is fired, a building falls). Each cue has several alternative lines, and the game plays one of them, so the chatter does not repeat itself too quickly.
- Every line is also shown as a subtitle. Subtitles and names are the only text in the game, so every line is short enough to read at a glance: twelve words at most.
- Each side hears its own show. **The human squads hear military radio** (93 lines). **The kaiju player hears a live Channel 9 news broadcast about its own rampage** (82 lines). The hearing on the end screen is heard by everyone (6 lines).
- Line names follow one pattern: `v_`, the cue name with dots turned into underscores, then a number. The same name is used for the sound file.

The game has four human squads. Three of them have a voice of their own (Granite, Beacon and Tempest), and the game prefers a squad's own voice for that squad's own actions. The fourth squad borrows from the shared lines.

## Tone rules

- Flight-combat radio melodrama, deadly serious. The world is absurd and nobody in it notices.
- Nobody winks and nobody jokes. The comedy is the gravity.
- A shot that misses the creature and hits the city should make its commander feel the paperwork coming.
- Nothing mean-spirited. No real people, places, brands or existing monsters.

## Rules for writing new lines

Anyone adding or changing lines should keep to these, because the voices are generated by simple offline speech synthesizers and checked by speech recognition.

- Twelve words or fewer (twenty for the hearing).
- Plain words. Numbers written out ("ten seconds", "Channel Nine"). No abbreviations, no contractions, and no punctuation except periods and commas.
- Avoid words that can be said two ways, because a synthesizer has to guess: live, lead, read, close, wind, record, present, object, minute as in tiny.
- Never name the creature, the city or a landmark. The humans say "the target" or "it"; the anchor says "the creature". That way every line works for all four kaiju and any map.
- Avoid words that only fit one faction ("tanks", "shells", "artillery"). Say "units", "shots", "a strike".
- Lines for a moment anyone could have caused (a building falling) must not say who caused it.

## How the script was checked

- Every line was checked by a script: valid data, unique names, every cue covered, word limits, only letters, periods and commas, no words that can be said two ways, and every word present in the pronunciation dictionary the speech tools use.
- Word counts: ordinary lines run from 2 to 12 words, 9.3 on average. Hearing lines run from 15 to 17 words.
- Every line was read aloud by three different offline speech synthesizers and the sound was fed to a speech recognizer, to find lines a machine voice cannot say clearly. Lines that came back garbled were rewritten.
- Speaking time, measured across the three synthesizers: an ordinary line takes 3.2 seconds on average and 5.3 at most; a hearing line takes 4.1 to 6.6 seconds; the three briefing lines together take 7 to 10 seconds before pauses and radio effects are added.
- Known weak spot: the word "volley" and the callsigns "Granite" and "Beacon" are often misheard by the speech recognizer (as "granted", "he can" and similar). They are clear to a person, and the subtitle is always there, but the two-word line "Volley away." scores badly in machine checks for that reason.
